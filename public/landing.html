§ 01 — Method

Four steps. The first three happen in the field, the last on your laptop. No app to babysit. No power bill. No service interval.

01
The Node Listens.
Every node carries a suite of micro-instruments — soil moisture, temperature, ambient humidity, light intensity, pH. It wakes briefly, samples, sleeps. Solar harvested at canopy height tops up a small battery that has, statistically, never been the failure point. Sample · 5 min  //  Sleep current · < 12 µA
02
The Mesh Relays.
LoRa radio carries each reading from neighbor to neighbor — peer-to-peer, no cell tower required. A signal at the back of a 40-acre plot can hop seven times before it leaves the field. If a node fails, the mesh re-routes around it on its own. Range · 1.4 km LoS  //  Hops · self-healing
03
The Gateway Delivers.
One master node — usually mounted at the highest point of the property — bundles the day's traffic and ships it skyward. Cellular, satellite, or local WiFi, depending on where you are. Encrypted in transit, signed at rest. Uplink · LTE-M / Iridium / WiFi  //  TLS 1.3
04
You See.
Raw readings become a living map. Patches of stress show up in warm color before they show up in your eyes. Compare years, set thresholds, export to your agronomist. The field finally fits on one screen. Latency · < 90s field-to-pixel  //  History · 5y retained

Each node is a small instrument box. Replaceable sensor cassettes, calibrated against laboratory reference. Update or extend the suite without leaving the field.

§ 05 — Autonomy

A 1.4 W photovoltaic cap charges a tiny LiFePO₄ cell that outlives the seasons. The node sleeps 99.6% of the time. The rest is listening, hopping, archiving.

The Sun Feeds It.
The Battery Holds the Night.

A node mounted on day one will still be transmitting in year seven. The cell is sized for two weeks of darkness — more than any winter can throw at it. There is no maintenance window, no swap cycle. Only the slow, accumulating record.

When the firmware needs an update, the gateway pushes it overnight. When a sensor cassette ages, you carry a fresh one out in your pocket and click it in. The node does not stop. The node has never stopped.

§ 06 — Deployments

The same node, two postures. Outdoors it lives off the sun. Indoors it taps the greenhouse's own current — and quietly takes over the chores that used to be done by hand.

01 · Outdoor
Plantations
& Fields
Solar-fed nodes spread across rows, vines, orchards — sun charges by day, the cell carries the night. No trenching, no wiring. Set them once and let the field speak for years.
Solar-poweredMesh · up to 7 hops
02 · Protected
Greenhouses
Wired into the greenhouse's own circuit — no solar needed under the roof. The node reads the air and talks to the ventilation: when temperature climbs, it cracks the vents open the exact amount to bring it back, then closes them again. The work that used to be done by hand, walking the rows with a thermometer, now happens on its own.
